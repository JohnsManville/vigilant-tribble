## 3. Loan and note status

Neither of Nancy's letters addresses the CMG loan. Any agreement must state
the following plainly.

**3.1 Current status.** Nancy and I are both obligors on the CMG note and both
appear on title. Nancy was added at refinance for rate and deduction purposes.

**3.2 Recording the deed does not release Nancy from the note.** Conveying her
ownership interest transfers title only. She remains liable to CMG on the note
until the loan is refinanced or paid off. Any agreement should say so, so that
no one is operating on a different understanding later.

**3.3 Refinance.** I intend in good faith to refinance and remove Nancy from
the note when the property and my finances support it. I am not agreeing to a
date certain, and nothing in this agreement should condition the deed
recording on a refinance occurring.

**3.4 During escrow.** Escrowed funds are to be disbursed to CMG monthly on
the due date. The account must remain current for the entire escrow period.
If an escrowed payment is missed or the account goes delinquent, the deed
records immediately and the remaining obligations under this agreement
survive.

**3.5 To be confirmed by Nancy's counsel:**

insurance claim would fund, and agreed to pay the difference. That agreement
was made directly between Nancy and me and was not documented in writing.

Any agreement resolving the property should also resolve this. I propose:

**4.1** The difference between the insurance-funded allowance and the actual

supplier invoices, and contractor billing, and provided to both sides.

**4.2** That amount is either paid at or before recording, or credited against

**4.3** If credited, the number of escrowed payments under the selected option
increases by the equivalent amount.

Section 4.3 is the practical point. Nancy's August 30 letter removed six
escrowed payments. If the finish delta is owed to me, the two net against each
other and much of the gap closes without anyone writing a check.

## What the ladder is doing

The three options are priced to be roughly equal to you, so Nancy picks the
currency she pays in: money (Option A) or time (Option C). That is a stronger
structure than demanding the twelve months back, because there is no single
number for them to negotiate down.

**Option A is the trap, and it is a fair one.** It is Nancy's own August 11
proposal, unchanged. Her counsel cannot easily call it unreasonable. If they
take it, you get the full twelve payments. If they refuse it, they are
refusing terms they authored three weeks ago, and that is worth something the
next time they describe a position as settled.

**Option C is the one that actually helps you most.** An October 1, 2026
vacate lines up with your October renovation target. It gets you into the
house, unblocks the Malcolm sell or rent decision, and removes the double
mortgage risk entirely. The six escrowed payments you give up are the cheapest
thing in this document compared to carrying two mortgages into 2027.

If they counter, the trade to protect is the vacate date, not the payment
count.

## The finish allowance changes the math

This is the most useful thing you told me. Section 4.3 lets the delta net
against the escrowed payments. If the delta is meaningful, Options B and C get
much closer to Option A in real dollars without Nancy having to fund more
months.

Two cautions, stated once:

**It is undocumented.** A personal agreement between the two of you, with no
writing. Its enforceability is a question for your attorney, not me. What is
true regardless is that right now is when it is easiest to memorialize,
because she wants something from you.

**Putting it through counsel converts it.** It stops being a family
understanding and becomes a claim in a negotiation. That is consistent with
the hard anchor posture you picked, and you should know that is what section 4
does. If you would rather keep it between you and Nancy, say so and I will
pull section 4 into a separate direct conversation and leave the counter
silent on it.

To make section 4 usable, someone has to produce a number: insurance-funded
allowance versus actual finish cost, from the claim scope, supplier invoices,
and contractor billing. The Roswell archive on your machine has folders for
the claim, the rebuild, and financials. Say the word and I will pull the delta.
