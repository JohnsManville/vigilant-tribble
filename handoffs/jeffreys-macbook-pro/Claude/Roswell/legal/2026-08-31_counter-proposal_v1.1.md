## 3. Loan and note status

Neither of Nancy's letters addresses the CMG loan. Any agreement must state
the following plainly.

**3.1 Current status.** Nancy and I are both obligors on the CMG note and both
appear on title. Nancy was added at refinance for rate and deduction purposes.

**3.2 Recording the deed does not release Nancy from the note.** Conveying her
ownership interest transfers title only. She remains liable to CMG until the
loan is refinanced or paid off. The agreement should say so, so no one is
operating on a different understanding later.

**3.3 Refinance.** I intend in good faith to refinance and remove Nancy from
the note when the property and my finances support it. I am not agreeing to a
date certain, and the deed recording is not conditioned on a refinance
occurring.

**3.4 During escrow.** Escrowed funds are disbursed to CMG monthly on the due
date and the account remains current for the full escrow period. If a payment
is missed or the account goes delinquent, the deed records immediately and the
remaining obligations survive.

**3.5 To be confirmed by Nancy's counsel:**
- Loan number and current servicer of record.
- Exact monthly payment, and whether it includes taxes and insurance. I hold

- Current principal balance and whether the account is current.

  any due on sale provision is implicated by recording.
- Accounting for the CMG escrow disbursement of **$8,837.38** received

# STRATEGY NOTE
**Not part of the counter proposal. Do not transmit this section.**

## The ladder

Three options priced to be roughly equal to you, so Nancy picks the currency
she pays in: money (A) or time (C). Stronger than demanding twelve months
back, because there is no single number for them to negotiate down.

**Option A is the trap, and it is a fair one.** It is Nancy's own August 11
proposal, unchanged. Her counsel cannot easily call it unreasonable. If they
take it, you get twelve payments. If they refuse it, they are refusing terms
they authored three weeks ago.

**Option C helps you most.** An October 1, 2026 vacate lines up with your
renovation target. It gets you in the house, unblocks the Malcolm sell or rent
decision, and removes the double mortgage risk. The six payments you give up
are the cheapest thing in this document next to carrying two mortgages into
2027. If they counter, protect the vacate date, not the payment count.

## Section 5 is the part that can break

You asked for every dollar accounted for, your time, and compensation. It is
in there. Three things you need to know before it goes out.

**Your documentation is 28 percent.** Your own substantiation file says
$9,235.38 of $32,659.12 is backed by a receipt. Opposing counsel will ask, and
72 percent of the claim currently has no paper. Missing items include $8,269
in appliances, $4,599.76 in cabinets, and an $8,840 Mold Masters payment with
no invoice on file. There is also an unresolved $546.93 double-entry that, if
it is a duplicate, means the figure is overstated. Assert a number you cannot
document and the credible parts get dragged down with it. The appliance and
cabinet receipts are recoverable from Home Depot order history. That is the
highest-value hour you can spend before this is sent.

**The categories are not equally strong.** Ranked:

1. *Finish allowance (5.2)* — strongest. She agreed to it. Undocumented, but
   it is a specific promise about a specific thing.
2. *GC services (5.3)* — strong on the merits. You performed work that has a
   market price. Weak on records: you need hours.
3. *Costs advanced (5.1)* — mixed. You are buying this property. Money you put
   into your own asset is not automatically a bill to Nancy. The defensible
   slice is what she agreed to, what she benefited from as occupant, and what
   she was obligated to carry and did not.
4. *HVAC (5.4)* — weakest as a claim. Installed years ago for her comfort, no
   agreement to repay, and it stays with the house you are keeping. Keep it in
   the accounting for basis and for the record. Do not lean on it.

**Section 5 changes what this negotiation is.** The RC draft was about
adjusting transfer terms. With Section 5 it becomes a money claim against your
mother, transmitted by attorneys. Consequences, stated once and then I will
drop it: the finish allowance stops being a family understanding and becomes a
legal claim; a release you sign without Section 5 satisfied wipes out the
claim, which is why 7.9 is conditioned; and this is likely to slow the deal
down rather than speed it up. You picked hard anchor, so that is what I built.
Say the word and I will produce a version with Section 5 pulled out and
handled directly between you and Nancy instead.
