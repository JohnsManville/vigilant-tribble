## 5. The part Jeff actually wants, and what the law does with it

Jeff's instruction was compensation for what his mother and his blood family put him through. He is entitled to have that stated accurately rather than dressed up, so here it is.

**5.1 There is no cause of action for how a family treated you.** Favoritism, coldness, being the one who always gets asked, being believed about the worst thing said about you, none of that is actionable in Oregon or anywhere else. It is the reason for the letter. It is not the reason for a number.

**5.2 Emotional distress.** Oregon's intentional infliction standard requires conduct that is an extraordinary transgression of the bounds of socially tolerable conduct. That bar is set high on purpose and ordinary family cruelty does not clear it. Negligent infliction generally needs physical injury or a special legally protected interest. **Not asserted. Asserting it would cost credibility on the claims that are real.**

**5.3 The "unsafe" and "manic" statements.** Defamation in Oregon carries a one year limitation under ORS 12.120. The June 2025 statements are outside it. Anything said within the last twelve months might not be, but it would still need publication to a third party, provable falsity, provable damage, and it would have to survive the conditional privilege that generally covers family members talking to each other about a family member's welfare. **Preserved, not asserted.** Jeff's medical clearance is documented twice and stays in the file.

**5.4 The company matter.** The FMLA vote-out is a different set of parties, a different transaction, and different limitations. Raising it inside a property negotiation with his mother weakens the property claim and gains nothing. **Out of this document entirely.**

**5.5 So where does the compensation actually live.** In Section 3. Every hour, every dollar, every improvement, and the full value of what he built, claimed without apology and without discount for the fact that she is his mother. **That is the honest version of the instruction: he is not paid for the suffering, he is paid for the work, and he stops donating the work.** The suffering is why he stopped donating it.

## 6. Risks that come with this posture, stated plainly

**6.1 ORS 124.100.** An elderly person, or her attorney-in-fact, can sue for financial abuse, and a prevailing plaintiff gets three times economic damages, three times non-economic damages, and attorney fees. Nancy is elderly. Her attorney-in-fact is her brother Patrick. Nothing Jeff has done is financial abuse. But a six figure demand from a son, delivered weeks after his mother moved out, is the raw material someone builds that claim from. **Mitigations: it comes from counsel, not from Jeff; every figure is documented before it is stated; the personal letter and this document do not arrive in the same week; and nothing in Jeff's own voice threatens her.**

**6.2 The labor reversal.** Jeff has said in writing, more than once, that he never planned to bill for his labor. Section 3.3 bills $88,064 for it. Dale will find that sentence. The defensible position is not to deny it: it is that the gift was to a mother in a continuing family arrangement, and when she converted that arrangement into a lawyered arm's length transfer, the basis for the gift ended. That argument works. It works far better if Jeff stops repeating the old sentence everywhere else.

**6.3 The unwritten agreements cut both ways.** The finish allowance is unwritten, which is why 3.4 pleads unjust enrichment in the alternative. The same weakness applies to the original "when you pass, it's ours." Which is the underlying reason to take the deed back rather than argue about it.

**6.4 The open Safeco claim.** Nothing in this or in any letter should put occupant knowledge, causation, or fault in writing while that claim is open.

**6.5 The double count.** $8,837.38, unresolved. Until it is, no figure from Section 3 leaves the file.
