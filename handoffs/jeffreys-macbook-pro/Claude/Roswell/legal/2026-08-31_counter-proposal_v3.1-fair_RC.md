## Note to counsel

Same three corrections as v3.0. What changes here is the money, and it changes in Nancy's favor.

The test applied to every line below is simple: **would a neutral third party, with no stake and no sympathy, look at this line and say that is fair?** Anything that failed that test is gone. What is left is defensible without argument, which is a different and better thing than being arguable.

Three deliberate concessions make this version work.

1. **Labor is claimed at the friends-and-family rate, not market.** Jeff publishes three rates: $128 standard, $149 drain and toilet, and **$99 friends and family**. His mother is family. Applying his own posted family rate is not a discount he invented for this negotiation, it is the rate that has always applied to her. That single choice takes the labor claim from $88,064 to **$68,112** and it does so on a principle rather than a concession.
2. **No cash is requested.** Not one dollar beyond what Nancy already offered on August 11. Everything established below is recited as consideration for the conveyance, not billed. She writes no check.
3. **What Jeff owes is netted first, not last.** The $38,921.38 outstanding to her comes off the top of the presentation, not buried at the bottom of a schedule.

**The result: a document that establishes roughly $82,000 of net value delivered and asks for $0.** That is a harder thing to argue with than a demand, and it is also the truth.
