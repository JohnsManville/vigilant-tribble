## 4. 🔴 Section 5.1 contradicts your tax position

The same casualty file takes this position for Form 4684:

> Beneficial owner = Jeff. Nancy is on title ONLY to qualify for a lower
> primary-residence mortgage rate; she contributed **$0** to purchase and pays
> **nothing** toward maintenance/upkeep. Benefits-and-burdens, Jeff is the tax
> owner.

You cannot tell the IRS you are the sole beneficial owner who bore 100% of the
costs, and tell Nancy's attorney that she owes you for those same costs. Those
are opposite claims about the same dollars.

- **5.1 (costs advanced) conflicts.** It is the claim that breaks.
- **5.2 (finish delta) survives.** A specific promise about a specific thing,
  independent of who owns the property.
- **5.3 (GC services) survives.** A service performed has a price whether or
  not you own the asset.
- **5.4 (HVAC) conflicts** for the same reason as 5.1.

The casualty file already flags the related tension as "the biggest tax
question": financed as Nancy's primary residence, treated as Jeff's
business-use casualty. Adding a reimbursement demand against Nancy makes that
harder to defend, not easier.

## Recommendation

**Do not send Section 5 as drafted.** Restructure to:

1. **Drop 5.1 and 5.4** as claims. Keep the numbers in the accounting for basis
   and for the record, not as a bill to Nancy.
2. **Keep 5.2**, but soften from a stated amount to a documented request: get
   the Safeco scope, then price it.

4. **Decide about the $59,771 before demanding an accounting**, not after. If
   the plan is still to repay it from insurance proceeds, an accounting demand
   may simply accelerate a conversation you would rather have on your own
   schedule.
